Skin - Dermatitis

10/02/2025 – Active

Shari O'Neill, DVM

FINDING- Dermatitis is a general term for skin inflammation that can have many causes and forms (such as an allergy to fleas, food, or environmental allergens, or other underlying conditions which may or may not be known). Signs of dermatitis include persistent scratching, areas of hair loss, thickening/ darkening of the skin, excessive dander/shedding, and open wounds which can be painful. Treatments include regular flea preventives, and medication to soothe itching and treat any secondary infections (which may include topical treatments, and/or oral or injectable medications). Dermatitis often requires several weeks or months of treatment to see improvement and is typically managed and not cured. Food trials, allergy testing and/or and medications that may require several weeks to take effect are not practical to induce in the shelter setting, and are better managed post-adoption. Adopters should follow-up with their veterinarian for further recommendations regarding this condition as ongoing treatment is likely to be required and recurrence is common depending on the underlying cause.

Cardiovascular - Heart Murmur IV-VI

08/04/2025 – Active

Shari O'Neill, DVM

FINDING- HEART MURMUR GRADE 4-6): Murmurs are abnormal heart sounds which can be caused by different structures both inside and outside the heart. Murmurs can be benign, but more often (especially in older dogs) can be associated with developing heart disease. The grade (or loudness of the murmur) is only sometimes related to the severity of the heart abnormality causing it. Grading is subjective because it is based on how it sounds to the listener and can change over time. Heart sounds may also be difficult to hear well in dogs who are overweight, panting, trembling, vocalizing or if there is ambient noise in the examination area. MV prioritizes providing echocardiograms (ultrasound of the heart) in house, or providing support for further diagnostics post-adoption, for dogs with higher grade murmurs. Exams are dependent on the availability of the cardiologist. Adopters should follow-up with their veterinarian for further recommendations regarding monitoring or diagnostics which may include radiographs (x-rays), an echocardiogram (if not already completed), or a referral to a veterinary cardiologist.
