HEART MURMUR (GRADE 4-6)- This dog was observed to have a heart murmur, which essentially is an abnormal heart sound caused by different structures inside and outside the heart. POOR GROOMING- Most dogs require some degree of grooming, which includes brushing, clipping/trimming the haircoat, bathing, cleaning the ears, and trimming nails. Inadequate grooming can lead to pain and discomfort. Long nails may make walking uncomfortable or difficult and ingrown nails can cause painful wounds. 2. Dermatitis

Dermatitis is a general term for skin inflammation that can have many causes and forms. Signs of dermatitis include persistent scratching, areas of hair loss, thickening/ darkening of the skin, excessive dander/shedding, and open wounds which can be painful. Chronic skin conditions can be caused by poor grooming, allergies, fleas, or other systemic disease. Treatments include regular flea preventives, and medication to soothe itching and treat and secondary infections (which may include topical treatments, and/or oral or injectable medications). Dermatitis often requires several weeks or months of treatment to see improvement. Successful treatment of chronic skin conditions requires treating the underlying cause. Many skin conditions are not cured but rather managed for the lifetime of the dog. Adopters should follow-up with their veterinarian for further recommendations regarding this condition as ongoing treatment is likely to be required and recurrence is common depending on the underlying cause.

Ocular- Cataract

09/11/2025 – Active

Shari O'Neill, DVM

DIAGNOSIS- A cataract is an opacity in the lens which can lead to vision loss. If the entire lens is involved, the eye will be blind. There are many causes of cataracts including inflammation, systemic conditions and aging. A small cataract that does not restrict vision is probably not significant. A more complete cataract may warrant treatment. A cataract can luxate, which means that it can slip from the tissue strands that hold it in place and can block the eye’s natural fluid drainage, which could result in glaucoma (a buildup in eye pressure) leading to pain and permanent blindness. A cataract can also cause glaucoma when it absorbs fluid and swells so as to partially obstruct fluid drainage. Cataract treatment generally involves surgical removal or physical dissolution of the cataract under anesthesia. This is invasive, costly, and is not considered unless it can restore vision or resolve pain. Pets with one normal eye and the other with a cataract can still see with their good eye and may not need surgery. MV does not refer dogs for cataract surgery while in care. Adopters should follow-up with their veterinarian for further recommendations regarding management of cataracts.

Cardiovascular - Heart Murmur IV-VI

09/11/2025 – Active

Shari O'Neill, DVM

FINDING- HEART MURMUR GRADE 4-6): Murmurs are abnormal heart sounds which can be caused by different structures both inside and outside the heart. Murmurs can be benign, but more often (especially in older dogs) can be associated with developing heart disease. The grade (or loudness of the murmur) is only sometimes related to the severity of the heart abnormality causing it. Grading is subjective because it is based on how it sounds to the listener and can change over time. Heart sounds may also be difficult to hear well in dogs who are overweight, panting, trembling, vocalizing or if there is ambient noise in the examination area. MV prioritizes providing echocardiograms (ultrasound of the heart) in house, or providing support for further diagnostics post-adoption, for dogs with higher grade murmurs. Exams are dependent on the availability of the cardiologist. Adopters should follow-up with their veterinarian for further recommendations regarding monitoring or diagnostics which may include radiographs (x-rays), an echocardiogram (if not already completed), or a referral to a veterinary cardiologist.

Skin - Dermatitis

09/11/2025 – Active

Shari O'Neill, DVM

FINDING- Dermatitis is a general term for skin inflammation that can have many causes and forms (such as an allergy to fleas, food, or environmental allergens, or other underlying conditions which may or may not be known). Signs of dermatitis include persistent scratching, areas of hair loss, thickening/ darkening of the skin, excessive dander/shedding, and open wounds which can be painful. Treatments include regular flea preventives, and medication to soothe itching and treat any secondary infections (which may include topical treatments, and/or oral or injectable medications). Ocular- Cataract DIAGNOSIS- A cataract is an opacity in the lens which can lead to vision loss. If the entire lens is involved, the eye will be blind. There are many causes of cataracts including inflammation, systemic conditions and aging. A small cataract that does not restrict vision is probably not significant. A more complete cataract may warrant treatment. A cataract can luxate, which means that it can slip from the tissue strands that hold it in place and can block the eye’s natural fluid drainage, which could result in glaucoma (a buildup in eye pressure) leading to pain and permanent blindness. A cataract can also cause glaucoma when it absorbs fluid and swells so as to partially obstruct fluid drainage. Cataract treatment generally involves surgical removal or physical dissolution of the cataract under anesthesia. This is invasive, costly, and is not considered unless it can restore vision or resolve pain. Pets with one normal eye and the other with a cataract can still see with their good eye and may not need surgery. MV does not refer dogs for cataract surgery while in care. Adopters should follow-up with their veterinarian for further recommendations regarding management of cataracts. Active
Dental /Periodontal Disease (Severe) FINDING- DENTAL DISEASE, SEVERE: Severe dental disease (Stage 3-4 periodontal disease), means greater than 50% of the tooth’s attachments are lost and they must be extracted. Untreated dental disease can also lead to jaw fractures, abscesses, oronasal fistulas, and increased risk of damage to internal organs. Many dogs arrive at Muttville with severe dental disease due to lack of preventive care for an extended period of time and may require extensive oral surgery to address their condition(s). Muttville prioritizes providing dental procedures for all dogs found to have severe dental disease. Active

Murmurs can be benign, but more often (especially in older dogs) can be associated with developing heart disease. The grade (or loudness of the murmur) is only sometimes related to the severity of the heart abnormality causing it. Grading is subjective because it is based on how it sounds to the listener and can change over time. Heart sounds may also be difficult to hear well in dogs who are overweight, panting, trembling, vocalizing or if there is ambient noise in the examination area.
